How it works

Intake and selection at your land trust, step by step. For today's figures, see the Hub.

The steps

Screener comes in › add it to a project › close applications › pre-screen › lock the lists › draw › invite › full application › certify › offer a home › hand off to CLT Stewardship

1Screeners come in works now

Applicants › Applications › view Not in a project yet

  1. People fill in an online screener whenever they like. The general screener isn't tied to a project; a screener made for one project can add its applications to that project automatically (see the tip below).
  2. Within a minute, the screener intake flow adds them here: a Household (named after the primary applicant), its members, and an Application with stage Screener submitted and all their answers. The application's Source form shows which online form it came from.
  3. Someone who applied before (same email) keeps their household and gets a new application.
  4. If they edit their screener later, the application updates, until Unique IDs are assigned. After that, the change isn't applied: the submission shows under Form problems so staff can compare it with the application.

If an entry can't be processed, it shows under Messages › Form submissions with status Needs attention and the reason.

Adding another screener form (for example one for a single project): in Cognito, copy the existing screener (Forms, the form's menu, Copy) so the questions keep their names, and paste the same flow web address into its Submission Settings (Post JSON Data to a Website, on Submit and on Update). Its first entry adds it under Settings › Source forms. To send its applications straight into a project, open that row and fill in Add new applications to project; to have that from the very first entry, add the row yourself beforehand (Forms platform Cognito Forms, Form ID = the number in the form's Cognito web address). If you reword or add questions, tell Unwind so the flow can be checked.

2Set up a project works now

Projects › New project

The page walks through six steps. Nothing is saved until the last one, so you can go back and forth freely.

  1. The basics: a name (for example Maple Court Phase 2), new homes or a resale, lottery or first come, first served, and when applications open and close.
  2. The homes: tick the properties in this project. Bedrooms come from the property. A home that isn't listed needs adding under Stewardship › Portfolio › Properties first.
  3. Price and monthly cost: the down payment, the loan term and the interest rate; then, for each home size, the sale price and what every buyer pays each month (property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, ground lease fee, reserve fee). Stu works out the mortgage payment and adds the rest to get the monthly housing cost, which the pre-screen uses for the housing ratio and the total debt ratio. If lower-income buyers can get a lower interest rate, or homes are priced by the buyer's income, select Add an income band: each band gets its own rate (and its own prices if you tick that box), and the pre-screen tests each household at the cost for its band.
  4. The rules: your organization's rules are shown beside a box for this project. Fill in only what differs, for example a lower max % of AMI. The pre-screen buffers are here too: screener answers are rough, so a buffer lets someone a little over a limit through to be checked properly at certification. Income buffer is a percent over the limit (10 turns an 80% limit into 88%). The two ratio buffers are points added to the limit (2 turns 35% into 37%).
  5. Who fits which home: which household sizes can apply for each home size, and their tier. Fill in a common starting point gives you a grid to adjust.
  6. Review and create: check the summary, then Create project. Stu makes the project, its homes and its priorities together and opens it.

To change a project afterwards, open it under Projects › Projects: everything the page asked is on its form, with the homes and priorities on their own tabs.

3Add applications to the project works now

Projects › Add to a project

  1. Pick the project at the top.
  2. Filter the screeners: not in a project yet (the default) or all of them, source form, county, home size wanted, household size, income, submission dates, first-time buyer.
  3. Tick the ones joining, or the box in the header to tick everyone shown, then select Add ticked. Add everyone shown adds every row that matches, in one go.

For a few at a time, you can also tick rows in any Applications view, select Edit, pick the Project and Save.

One application belongs to one project. Once it has a Unique ID it's locked to that project. If a household is also interested in a later project, it fills in the screener again then.

4Close applications and assign Unique IDs works now

Projects › Buyer Selection

Buyer Selection is the page to keep open while a project is being selected. Pick the project and it shows the project at a glance across the top (applications, eligible, on a list, invited, full applications in, past their date), then every step from set-up to invitations, which ones are done, and the one to do next. Once households are invited, the last step lists each one with its due date and whether its full application is in, still to come or overdue. A step's button only appears when the steps before it are finished.

Once applications have closed, select Close applications and assign Unique IDs. Stu numbers the applications ID-0001, ID-0002 and so on, in the order the screeners were first submitted, and freezes their answers. The lists only ever show these IDs, never names.

5Pre-screen works now

Projects › Pre-screen

The pre-screen tests what each applicant said on the screener against the project's rules, and the organization's where the project leaves one blank. It decides who goes into the lottery. Nothing is verified yet: certification checks the same rules again later.

  1. Pick the project. Every application in it is tested straight away and shown with its result: Eligible for selection, Not eligible or Needs review. Nothing is saved yet.
  2. Select a result to see every test behind it, with the applicant's figure and the limit.
  3. If a yellow line says the project is missing priorities or a monthly housing cost, open the project, fill them in, and come back.
  4. Select Save results. Each application gets its result, its reasons, the home sizes it's eligible for, and one saved check per test. Its stage moves to Pre-screened.
  5. Run it again any time before the lists lock, for example after late screeners arrive or a rule changes. Saved results are replaced.

The tests: income limit (with the project's income buffer: 10% over the limit unless you change it), minimum income, first-time buyer, local tie, primary residence, asset limit, credit range, bankruptcy, and whether the household size fits a home size they asked for. Then, for each size that fits, the housing ratio (monthly cost ÷ monthly income) and the total debt ratio (monthly cost plus stated monthly debt ÷ monthly income). A household only needs one home size to work.

To override a result (for example Needs review to Eligible): open the application, change Pre-screen result, tick Pre-screen overridden, and write who, when and why in Pre-screen notes. The pre-screen then leaves that application alone.

Income over the limit but inside the buffer passes, with a note. Stated income is rough; certification uses verified income against the real limit.

6Build the lists, lock, draw and certify works now

Projects › Buyer Selection

  1. Build the lists. One list for each home size. Each eligible household goes on the list for every home size it is eligible for, in its tier. Anyone marked Needs review has to be decided first. Until the lists are locked you can build them again.
  2. Lock the lists. Type the Board Observer's name and select Lock the lists. Nothing on a list can change after this. Each list gets a check number worked out from its contents. Select Download the lists (PDF), print it, and have the observer initial it.
  3. Draw. In front of the observer, roll a ten-sided die ten times and type the ten digits in the order rolled. Read them back, then select Draw. Each household gets one lottery number, used on every list it is on, and each list is ranked by tier, then lottery number. The same roll always gives the same order, so anyone can check it. A draw can't be redone. (A first come, first served project has no dice: locking ranks each list by when households first applied.)
  4. Certify the results. Select Download the results (PDF), print it, have the observer and staff sign, file the signed copy in the project's documents, then select Mark the results certified.

If the page says the lists no longer match their check numbers, something changed an entry after the lock. The draw won't run. Tell whoever looks after Stu.

7Invite to the full application works now

Projects › Invite

Once the results are certified, the Invite page walks you through three steps. Stu doesn't send the email: you send each one from your own mail.

  1. Who to invite. Each list shows its households in rank order, with the next ones to invite already ticked: as many as the project's invites per list, less the ones already invited. Untick anyone you aren't inviting yet.
  2. The email. The first time, paste your full application form's web address. Stu adds each household's application number to the end of it, so their full application finds its way back to their record. Then read the message and change anything you like. Words in curly brackets, like {first name} and {link}, are filled in for each household. Keep this wording for next time saves your changes as the starting point for every later invitation (Settings › Message templates).
  3. Send. Stu shows one finished email at a time, with that household's own link in it. Copy the address, the subject and the message into your mail, or select Open in my mail app. Send it, then select Sent: mark as invited and go to the next. That changes the application's stage, starts its reply-by date, saves its link on it, and keeps a copy of the message under Messages › Notices. Skip for now leaves that household waiting.

When a household drops out or misses its date, open its entry on the ranked list and change its List status (Declined, Unresponsive, Ineligible). Come back to Invite and the next household on that list is ticked for you.

8Review and certify works now

Projects › Review and certify, or the Review button beside a household on Buyer Selection

When a household's full application is in, check it against its documents. The page lists every line the household entered and works the figures out as you go. Certification asks the same questions as the pre-screen, but of verified figures and with no buffers.

  1. The household. Check who will live in the home and set the certified household size.
  2. Income, debts and assets. For each line choose Accepted (what they entered is right), Adjusted (type what the documents show, and why) or Excluded (and why). Exclude any account your asset limit doesn't count, such as a retirement account.
  3. Documents. Under each line are the names of the files the household uploaded for it, or No documents uploaded. Files uploaded for the household as a whole, such as tax returns, are listed under Other documents. Files uploaded for the household as a whole are shown with the step they support: tax returns with income, credit reports with debts, proof of residence with the local tie. Open this household's documents opens their folder in your document share. Each line is labelled Income 1, Debt 1, Account 1 and so on, which is how that line's files start in the folder. Where the documents are is set once for your organization, so there is nothing to paste.
  4. Confirmed by staff. Tick each thing you have checked: first-time buyer, local tie, primary residence, homebuyer education. Only the ones your rules require are shown.
  5. The result. The certified income, its percent of AMI, and every test with how it was worked out. The monthly housing cost is the one for the income band the verified income falls in.
  6. Decide. Certify this household works once every line is reviewed and every test passes for at least one home size they are on a list for. Not certified needs a reason. Save and come back later keeps your work without deciding.

Certifying locks the figures, sets the date the certification is good until, saves every test with the certification, marks the household Eligible, and marks its place Approved on the lists for the home sizes it passed. To change anything afterwards, select Review again.

Stu doesn't write to the household. If they aren't certified, the reason you typed is on the certification for when you write to them. Documents stay in the forms platform for now: copying a certified household's documents into SharePoint is still to come.

9Offer a home and hand off coming later

Offer home checks the list order and starts a sale. When the purchase and sale agreement is signed, the household becomes an occupancy in CLT Stewardship.
